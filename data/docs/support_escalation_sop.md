# Support Escalation SOP — Aurora Atlas Events

This SOP tells event and support staff how client issues are triaged, when a CRM ticket must be opened, and when the #support-escalations Teams channel is used. It applies to the Events Support team, the registration desk and the on-site helpdesk. The goal is a predictable ladder: Tier 1 absorbs routine issues, Tier 2 handles what Tier 1 cannot, and genuine escalations reach management in minutes instead of days.

## 1. Triage and the ticket

Every client issue beyond a single factual answer is opened as a ticket in AtlasCRM, module Events Support, with a one-line summary and a priority of low, medium or high. The ticket is opened before any workaround is attempted, because the record matters even when the fix is quick. A ticket opened without the client name and event date is returned to the opener. The desk that first receives the issue owns the ticket until it is reassigned in AtlasCRM, never informally.

## 2. Response SLAs

High priority tickets must receive a first response within 4 business hours, medium priority within 1 business day, and low priority within 2 business days. First response means a human reply to the client, not an acknowledgement auto-email. Tier 1 resolves 70 percent of all tickets within 8 business hours of opening. Tier 2 resolves the remainder within 24 business hours. A ticket that misses its SLA is flagged amber in AtlasCRM, and two amber flags in a week are raised at the desk's daily stand-up.

## 3. Choosing the priority

High priority means a paying client is blocked or a public event is at risk: registration failures during a launch, payment gateway faults, or an on-site problem during an event. Medium priority means degraded service with a workaround, such as badge reprints queued at the desk or one broken agenda page. Low priority covers questions, cosmetic defects and enhancement wishes. When in doubt, pick the higher priority; desk leads downgrade more easily than they explain a missed SLA.

## 4. When to escalate to Tier 2

Escalate to Tier 2 when Tier 1 has worked a ticket for 8 business hours without resolution, when the fix needs admin rights in AtlasCRM, or when the issue repeats for the same client within 30 days. Escalation is a reassignment in AtlasCRM with a handover note describing what was tried; a ticket escalated without a note is bounced back. Tier 2 owns the ticket until resolution or a further escalation under section 5.

## 5. When to open a management escalation

Certain triggers skip the normal ladder and go straight to the duty manager: any suspected data incident, meaning customer data exposed to the wrong party, is reported to the Data Protection Officer within 1 hour of discovery; VIP client issues receive a 15-minute first response; refund disputes over INR 50,000 need a manager's sign-off before any commitment is made to the client. For these three triggers the duty manager is reached by phone call, not by waiting on a queue.

## 6. The #support-escalations Teams channel

The #support-escalations channel in Microsoft Teams is used exactly when one of the following is true: the ticket is high priority, the ticket has exceeded Tier 2 without resolution, or the ticket is one of the section 5 triggers. The message posted to the channel must contain the AtlasCRM ticket number, the event name, the client name, a one-sentence status and the next step with an owner. Do not paste client addresses, payment details or passport data into the channel; link the ticket instead. Posts without a ticket number are deleted by the channel moderator.

## 7. On-call and major incidents

The on-call rota covers event days and is published in AtlasCRM. A major incident, meaning more than one client affected by the same failure, gets an incident channel, a named incident lead and a status page update every 30 minutes until resolved. The post-incident review for a P1 major incident happens within 5 business days, chaired by the duty manager, with a written timeline and the corrective actions filed against the original ticket.

## 8. Records and reporting

All tickets keep their full history inside AtlasCRM; screenshots and emails are attached to the ticket, not kept in personal folders. Weekly reports show SLA attainment by priority and by desk. Individual performance conversations use these reports, which is one more reason to file tickets accurately at the moment an issue arrives rather than reconstructing them later.
